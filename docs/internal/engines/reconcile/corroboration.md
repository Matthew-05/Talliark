# Reconcile: nomination by parallel-column corroboration

Status: **implemented**, with the corpus injection harness still to be folded
into the scorer. This is the record for nomination: it replaces label-only
nomination as the primary route, and sharpens the rule that structure nominates
and arithmetic only ever confirms.
The original measurements below come from the prototype run
over the cell layer R-1 already publishes, on the Apple 10-K, the Carver Trust
statements and `Sample-Financial-Statements-1`.

## 1. The problem with nominating on a label

`label-total` is right about what it sees and blind to most of what matters. It
is reliable — a row opening with *Total* almost always is one — but a filing
states most of its totals without ever using the word:

> Ending balances · Cash generated by operating activities · Income before
> provision for income taxes · Weighted-average diluted shares · Provision for
> income taxes · Common stock outstanding, ending balance · Operating
> income/(loss)

Every one of those foots, and none is reachable by a lexicon. The way out is not
a longer lexicon or more signals stacked behind it: each new rule buys a narrow
slice of the corpus and brings its own false positives, and the ruling, outdent
and block-terminal signals together would still miss the rollforwards, which are
the most error-prone statements a reviewer reads.

## 2. The proposal

**Nomination proposes a row structure, not a cell.** A *structure* is one
hypothesis — *body rows r₁…rₖ foot to row t* — and it is evaluated independently
in every value column of the table. A financial statement prints its figures in
parallel: three periods, several segments, a cost/gain/loss/fair-value quartet.
Those columns share a row structure and share nothing else.

> **The signal.** The same rows footing to the same row in more than one
> independent column. A coincidental tie in one column is common; the same
> rows tying in two unrelated sets of figures is not.

This is the observation in Matthew's sketch: check whether the neighbours on the
same rows tie under the same pairing. For cross-footing the whole thing
transposes — a structure across columns, corroborated by other rows — so R-5
comes almost free.

### 2.1 Leave-one-out, and why it is the whole design

The obvious rule — *establish a structure when two or more columns foot exactly*
— has a defect that makes it useless for finding errors, and it is the defect
that matters most: **the column carrying the error is excluded from the evidence,
so it can veto its own examination.** A two-column structure with a mistake in
one column has one tie left, falls below the bar, and is thrown away silently.
The method would be blindest in exactly the place an error exists.

So the retention rule and the evidence rule are separated:

> **A structure is retained whenever it foots exactly in at least one column.** A
> column that fails to foot is never a reason to discard the structure, because
> that column is the thing being examined, not part of the evidence for it.
>
> **Each column is then judged on the evidence that excludes it.** For a column
> that missed this is automatic — it contributed nothing — so the question is
> only whether what remains proves the rows.

Measured, this is the difference between a scan that finds three quarters of the
errors in a document and one that finds nineteen twentieths (§4).

### 2.2 The rules

Let `A` be the number of **independent** columns footing exactly, and `L(c)` mean
the accused cell's own row label announces a total — its own, taken from the
nearest label column to its left, never column 0 (§6).

| | Test |
|---|---|
| **Confirm** column *c* (it ties) | `A ≥ 2`, or `A ≥ 1` and `L(c)` |
| **Accuse** column *c* (it misses) | `A ≥ 2`, or `A ≥ 1` and (`L(c)` or *c* foots exactly on another established structure in this table) |

`A` never counts a missing column, so the accusation test already rests on
evidence excluding the accused figure.

### 2.3 R1 still holds, and is sharper for it

R1 says a cell becomes a candidate total only on evidence **independent of its
value**. Corroboration satisfies that as written: the candidacy of the cell at
(row t, column A) rests on the arithmetic of columns B and C, which is
independent of what A contains.

> **R1′.** A column's own arithmetic may never nominate its own total. A row
> structure corroborated by *other* columns is evidence independent of this
> cell's value, and may.

Arithmetic still confirms rather than proposes; it simply proposes *structure*
now, from figures it is not judging.

## 3. What it finds

Apple 10-K, 80 pages, 52 tables.

| | |
|---|---|
| Blind hypotheses tested | 1,538 |
| Tie in exactly **one** column — the coincidence floor | **19** |
| Tie in **two or more** independent columns | **51** |
| Totals confirmed by corroboration | **157** |
| Confirmed by `label-total` today | 53 |
| Union of the two routes | **159** |
| Breaks on the clean filing | **0** |
| Search cost | 1,538 evaluations, well under a second |

The first two rows are the argument. If ties were coincidence, multi-column ties
would be far rarer than single-column ones. They are **2.7× more common**, which
is only possible if they are structural. The coincidence floor is visible,
measured, and sits an order of magnitude below the signal.

Every corroborated structure was read by hand against the filing. All are real
totals, including every example in §1. **22 of them are two-addend runs** — the
class the current policy refuses outright.

| Document | Confirmed today | With corroboration | Breaks on the clean document |
|---|---|---|---|
| Apple 10-K | 53 | **159** | 0 |
| Carver Trust | 17 | **46** | 0 |
| Sample Financial Statements | 2 | 2 | 0 |

## 4. It detects real errors, and accuses only the right column

159 errors injected, one per confirmed total, the scan re-run against each:

| Injected error | Detected in exactly that column | Silent | Wrong column accused |
|---|---|---|---|
| Digit transposition (+9) | **151 / 159 — 95%** | 8 | **0** |
| Single glyph (+100) | **151 / 159 — 95%** | 8 | **0** |
| Sign flip | **151 / 159 — 95%** | 8 | **0** |

Ten further breaks were raised across the runs. Every one is a **true subtotal
cascade** — the perturbed row is an addend of the total above it, which really is
wrong as a result. Zero were unexplained.

The 8 silent cases are structures left with a single tie, no total label on the
accused cell, and no other structure in the table where that column foots. They
are genuinely unprovable, and silence is the right answer.

Under the naive *two exact ties* rule the same experiment detects 113 of 159 —
72%. The leave-one-out rule is worth 38 real errors per filing.

## 5. The two routes are complementary, and the corpus says where

This does **not** replace `label-total`. Measured, they fail in opposite places:

| | Note tables | Primary statements |
|---|---|---|
| Shape | two columns, current and prior year | three columns, one per period |
| Labels | *Total deferred tax liabilities*, *Total lease liabilities* — richly labelled | *Ending balances*, *Cash generated by…* — no total word |
| `label-total` | **carries them** | misses most |
| Corroboration | **carries them, and with the label can accuse** | **carries them, and can accuse alone** |

18 of Apple's 52 tables have a single value column and are reachable only by
label. `Sample-Financial-Statements-1` yields no corroborated structure at all —
its balance sheet is two panels printed side by side and its other tables have
one column each — and depends on labels entirely. Run both routes; a structure
carrying both is the strongest evidence there is.

## 6. What it fixes for nothing

- **Caption rows.** Skipping them cost 6 false breaks when guarded only by
  labels. Under corroboration every one of those six — page 33 twice, page 43
  three times, page 46 — resolves *correctly*: the true two-row structures are
  found and confirmed across three columns. `SKIP_CAPTION_ROWS` can be turned on
  once corroboration is the guard.
- **The two-addend rule.** Corroboration is the second signal the policy has been
  waiting for, and it needs no rulings. `ruling-above` becomes a refinement
  rather than a first-pass dependency, which takes R-4 off the critical path.
- **The double-count trap.** A run containing its own subtotal does not tie in
  the sibling columns, so it is refused by evidence instead of by a special case.
  Matthew's own sketch shows this: 5 + 10 = 15 in the left column and 2 + 4 ≠ 4
  in the right, so the nested reading is rejected while 5 + 10 + 15 = 30 and
  2 + 4 + 4 = 10 both stand.
- **The unknown-block-top trap.** Page 47's *Total non-current portion of term
  debt* — the false break this work already had to fix once — is simply
  confirmed, across two columns.

## 7. The guards, and what each one is for

Each was written after a concrete failure or a measurement, not in advance.

| Guard | Why |
|---|---|
| **Independence** — identical or proportional columns count as one | A *% of total* column beside its amounts, or the same figures restated in another unit, is the same tie written twice. Cross-multiply the addend vectors; equal ratios throughout means one column, not two. |
| **Non-degenerate** — the total is non-zero and at least two addends are non-zero | 0 + 0 = 0 corroborates every structure ever proposed. |
| **The label is the cell's own** — taken from the nearest label column to its left | The prototype read it from column 0 and immediately accused `Sample-Financial-Statements-1`: on a two-panel balance sheet the left panel's *Total current assets* sat beside the right panel's income taxes payable, and the statement was reported as failing to add up. The cell layer already assigns the right label; the rule is to use it. |
| **Commensurability** — a column may be accused only where its printed decimal specificity matches the agreeing columns' and its header does not name a rate, an average or a per-unit amount | The RSU rollforward: its share counts foot, its *Weighted-Average Grant-Date Fair Value Per RSU* column never will. Header semantics is already Reconcile's to own (R9). |
| **Leave-one-out** — a column is judged on evidence that excludes it | R1′ made operational, and §2.1's whole point. |
| Existing decimal agreement, and the double-count refusal on the label route | Unchanged. |

## 8. What it costs, and what is still unknown

- **The break path is simulated.** No document in the corpus contains a real
  footing error, so 159 injections × 3 error shapes is what stands in for one.
  Three shapes agreeing at 95% is reassuring; a real erroneous filing is better.
- **One document supplies most of the evidence.** Apple has comparative
  statements throughout. The corpus needs a second filing with real three-period
  statements before these rates mean much beyond it.
- **8 totals stay unprovable** and always will: one tie, no label, no sibling
  structure. The remedy is a third column or a label, not a weaker guard.
- **Cross-footing** is the transpose and is not prototyped.
- **The search is O(n²·c) per table.** 1,538 hypotheses over 80 pages, sub-second.
  No budget concern, but it is quadratic in row count and a pathological table
  should be capped.

## 9. Where it lands

- `contracts/reconcile-v1.json` — one new member of the closed `SignalName`
  enum, `column-corroboration`. Nothing else changes: evidence is already carried
  per nominated cell and the sum tree already publishes the run. The contract
  changes **before** the code.
- `engines/reconcile/structures.py` — new. The search over row spans, the
  independence and degeneracy guards, and the per-column evaluation. Beside
  `sums.py` rather than inside it: `sums` answers *does this run add up*, and this
  answers *which runs are anyone's addends*.
- `nominate.py` — the new signal joins the registry; the policy layer reads
  independent agreeing columns where it reads a signal count today, which is what
  finally makes `two_addend_runs_publishable()` true.
- `labels.py` — `is_non_additive_column`, beside the header semantics it owns.
- `sums.py` — the confirm and accuse tests of §2.2, and `SKIP_CAPTION_ROWS`
  flipped on once corroboration is guarding it.
- `scripts/score_reconcile.py` — the injection harness becomes part of the
  scorer. On a corpus with no errors in it, a detection rate is the only honest
  way to measure the break path at all.

## 10. What depends on this

The sum tree no longer stands on the detected table grid; its substrate is an
alignment lattice built from value bounding boxes ([`README.md`](README.md)
§4.1). **That change was not safe without this one**, and landed after it: a
lattice is a looser substrate than a grid, and looseness under label-based
nomination means more spurious runs and more chances to accuse a document that
foots perfectly. Under corroboration it costs almost nothing.

## 11. Recommendation

Adopt corroboration as the **primary** nomination route with `label-total` as the
co-equal second, under leave-one-out and the guards in §7. On the Apple 10-K it
takes 53 confirmed totals to 159 at a strictly higher evidence standard, turns 22
two-addend structures from unpublishable into corroborated, detects 95% of
injected errors in three different shapes while accusing the wrong column zero
times, and removes `ruling-above` from the critical path.

The one thing not to trade away is that a break keeps resting on evidence other
than the figure it is accusing. Everything else here is negotiable on a number.
